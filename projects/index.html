Projects

Our lab studies how circadian timing is transformed across multiple levels of neural organization. We focus on four connected questions: how defined output pathways shape behavioral timing, how the central pacemaker differs across species, how multiple behavioral rhythms are organized across the day, and how circadian time changes the state and function of downstream neural circuits.

Where this is going

These four areas are closely connected. Our next goal is to understand how they interact: how differences in pacemaker dynamics are transformed by downstream pathways, how interacting output circuits generate and amplify biological rhythms, and how this organization breaks down in disease.

Current and developing directions include comparative SCN-output circuitry, local molecular clocks in neuromodulatory systems, hormonal and neurotransmitter feedback, and the loss of circadian organization in disorders affecting cognition, reward, and behavior.
